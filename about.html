I'm Yuval Elbar — a software engineer and independent security researcher. My day-to-day background is building production Python systems, and that engineering lens shapes how I hunt bugs: I read code the way it was written, find the one line that quietly breaks an invariant, and prove the impact.

In my own time I build a portfolio of CVEs and vulnerability writeups across major vendors, with a growing focus on low-level components and the intersection of AI and security. Modern AI tooling — agents, MCP gateways, ML checkpointing, inference runtimes — is being shipped fast, and the classic bug classes (path traversal, SSRF, command injection, unsafe deserialization, template injection) are showing up in brand-new places. That's the seam I like to work.

Everything here goes through coordinated vulnerability disclosure: reported privately, embargoed until a fix ships, and only then written up. My findings have been fixed by teams at Microsoft, Google, NVIDIA, IBM, NASA/JPL, Pydantic and others. Some of this research has been published in Digital Whisper (Hebrew) and via the Cycode research blog.
